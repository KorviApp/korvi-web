Who we are

Korvi OÜ decides how and why the personal data in this policy is used (the "controller" under the GDPR). Write to us about privacy at privacy@korvi.eu, or about anything else at hello@korvi.eu.

Korvi ("we", "us", "our") is a meal-planning and grocery-shopping app built for the Estonian market. This policy explains what data we collect, why, and how we handle it.

1. Data we collect

Account information: When you sign up, we store your email address, display name, and a hashed password. If you sign in with Google, Apple, or Facebook, we receive your name, email, and profile picture URL from that provider.

Profile picture: If you choose a picture from your phone as your profile picture, we store it and show it to the people in your household. You can change it at any time.

Household data: Meal plans, shopping lists, recipe preferences, the people your household cooks for, the foods each of them avoids, and your household's invite code. We keep this in our database so everyone in your household sees the same plan and list.

Recipes you add: The recipes you write or import, whether they're only for you, for your household, or shared with everyone. A recipe you share with everyone is reviewed by us before other people can see it.

Korvi Plus: If your household has Korvi Plus, we and RevenueCat (which handles subscriptions for us) keep the subscription's status, which store it was bought in, the product, the store's transaction IDs, and its trial, renewal and expiry dates. RevenueCat knows the subscription by your household's ID, not by your name or email. We never see or store your card or bank details: payments are handled by the App Store or Google Play.

Recipe reports: If you report a public recipe, we store which recipe it was, the reason you picked, any note you wrote, your account ID and when you reported it. Only Korvi's team can read reports. The recipe's author isn't told who reported it.

Usage analytics (only if you agree): The first time you open the app, Korvi asks whether you agree to usage analytics. PostHog (EU-hosted) doesn't start, and stores nothing on your phone, until you agree. If you agree, it records which screens are visited, navigation flows, feature usage, and when the app is installed, updated, opened or put in the background. Events are linked to your user ID but do not include personal content like meal plan details. To work, PostHog then keeps a small file on your phone holding a random ID for your installation and your device, your account ID once you sign in, timing for the current session, and events waiting to be sent. PostHog doesn't keep your IP address and doesn't record your screen. You can change your answer at any time in Settings.

Error reports: We use Sentry (EU-hosted) to capture app crashes and errors. Reports include the stack trace, device model, OS version, and your user ID so we can investigate and fix problems. They do not include personal content, and Sentry doesn't store your IP address.

Feedback: If you use the in-app feedback button, we store your message with your account ID, your phone's operating system and version, the app version, the screen you were on, and a screenshot of that screen. The screenshot shows whatever was on the screen (for example names or the foods someone avoids), so the feedback form tells you it's attached and lets you remove it before you send. We keep your feedback as an issue in our private GitHub repository. We don't store your email in it; if we reply, we look your email up from your account ID.

People you cook for and food you avoid

Korvi plans for everyone you cook for, not only the people with an account.

What we store. For each person: whether they're an adult or a child, an optional first name, and the foods they avoid, chosen from a fixed list of 14, the allergens EU law requires food labels to show: celery, crustaceans (such as prawns and crab), dairy, eggs, fish, gluten, lupin, molluscs (such as mussels and squid), mustard, nuts, peanuts, sesame, soy and sulphites. For someone with a Korvi account we use their account name instead of a separate one. We don't store dates of birth, surnames, photos or any other detail about people without an account. Nothing here is required: a person can be added without a name, and nobody has to have a list.

This is health information. A list of foods someone avoids can show an allergy or an intolerance, which the law treats as health data, and we handle it that way.

Why. To help leave out the foods the people you cook for avoid, whoever in your household is choosing. Korvi isn't a medical or allergy-safety tool: always check ingredients and labels yourself.

Legal basis. We process the foods people avoid on the basis of your explicit consent (GDPR Articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a)). The first time you add a food to avoid, for yourself or for someone else, the app asks you to agree before anything is saved, and we record that you agreed, when, and which wording you saw. When you add foods for someone else, you also confirm you're allowed to share their details (for a child, as their parent or carer). You can withdraw consent at any time by clearing the list in Settings, under Food you avoid: the foods are deleted straight away. Withdrawing doesn't affect what we did before you withdrew.

Who sees it. Everyone in your household can see each person's name, whether they're an adult or a child, and what they avoid, and any member can change it. That's the point of it: whoever picks tonight's meal, Korvi leaves out what anyone at the table avoids. Nobody outside your household can see it unless they join your household. If you move to a household that has other people in it, Korvi asks you first what to do with the foods you avoid and any people you added: "Share with this household" or "Clear instead". Nothing moves until you choose. The app also keeps a copy on your phone so it works offline.

Invite codes. Anyone who has your household's invite code or invite link can use it to join, and then sees everything above. Codes and links don't expire on their own, so only share them with people you want in your household. You can remove someone from your household at any time: they lose access straight away, and your household gets a new code and link, so the old ones stop working.

Analytics and error reports. If you've agreed to analytics, when a list is saved our analytics (PostHog) records that it was saved and two numbers: how many people in the household have a list, and how many foods are on those lists in total. It never receives names, which foods anyone avoids, or whether someone is an adult or a child. Like all our analytics, the record is linked to your account ID, not your name or email. Error reports (Sentry) don't include names or the foods anyone avoids.

If you told Korvi about food you avoid before this change. Until now your list was private: kept on your phone and copied to your account. The first time you open the updated app, it tells you that food you avoid is now shared with your household, shows your list, and gives you two choices:

  • Keep it and share it with my household. You agree as described under Legal basis, and your list moves onto your place in the household, where everyone in it can see it.
  • Clear my list instead. Your old list is deleted and nothing is shared.

Either way, both old private copies are deleted: the one on your phone and the one stored with your account. Until you choose, your list stays private and isn't shared. You can change or clear it at any time in Settings.

People without an account. Someone you add, such as a child or an adult who doesn't use Korvi, can use their rights through the household member who added them, who can change or remove their details at any time, or by emailing privacy@korvi.eu to see or delete what we hold about them.

2. Data we do not collect

We do not collect location data, contacts, call logs, SMS or advertising identifiers. We only look at your photos when you choose one as your profile picture. The camera is used only to read a household invite QR code, on your phone; no picture is kept. We never see or store your card or bank details: payments are handled by the App Store or Google Play. The only health information we store is the foods each person in your household avoids, which you choose to enter (see "People you cook for and food you avoid").

3. How we use your data

  • To provide and improve the meal-planning and shopping features
  • To sync meal plans and shopping lists between household members
  • To run Korvi Plus for your household
  • To send transactional emails (account confirmation, password reset)
  • To review public recipes and act on reports about them
  • To monitor app stability and fix crashes
  • To understand usage patterns and improve the app

Legal basis.

  • Your account, household data, recipes, Korvi Plus and account emails: to provide the service you signed up for (performance of a contract, GDPR Article 6(1)(b)).
  • The foods people avoid: explicit consent (see "People you cook for and food you avoid").
  • Usage analytics: your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), which you can give or withdraw at any time in Settings.
  • Error reports: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and secure (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Feedback: our legitimate interest in answering you and improving Korvi, because you chose to send it (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Reviewing public recipes and handling reports: our legitimate interest in keeping public recipes safe and lawful (Article 6(1)(f)), and our duties under the EU Digital Services Act (Article 6(1)(c)).
  • Records of household moves and our backups: our legitimate interest in being able to undo a mistaken move and recover from a failure (Article 6(1)(f)).

4. Where your data is stored

Our database is hosted on Supabase (AWS, EU region, Ireland). The people you cook for and the foods they avoid are stored there with the rest of your household data, and the database's own rules let only your household's members read or change them. Analytics is processed by PostHog in its EU region, and error reports by Sentry in its EU region.

Some of the services we use are based in the United States and can process personal data there: RevenueCat (Korvi Plus purchases), Expo (app updates), Resend (account emails), Anthropic (see below), GitHub (where we keep feedback) and Cloudflare (storage for recipe pictures and feedback screenshots). Each describes the safeguards it relies on for transfers from the EU, such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses, in its privacy policy, linked in section 5. If you sign in with Google, Apple or Facebook, that provider handles your sign-in under its own privacy policy.

6. Data retention

Your account data is kept as long as your account exists.

  • Removing a person from your household in the app deletes them, with their name and the foods they avoid, straight away.
  • Clearing someone's list deletes the foods they avoid straight away.
  • Leaving a household, joining another, or being removed by its owner deletes your place in the household you leave. If the household you move to has other people in it, Korvi asks what to do with the foods you avoid: "Share with this household" or "Clear instead". Nothing moves until you choose, and "Clear instead" deletes them. If you move to a new household of your own, they come with you. Recipes you wrote come with you. The household's list, meals and Regulars stay with it. We keep a record of which household an account left and when, so a mistaken move can be undone, and we delete that record 90 days after the move.
  • If that leaves the household with nobody in it, the people you added are handled the same way: if the household you move to has other people in it, Korvi asks whether to share them with it or clear them, and nothing moves until you choose; if you move to a new household of your own, they come with you. The emptied household is closed: nobody can open it any more, and 30 days after it closes it is deleted with everything still in it, including its meals, shopping lists and any people and foods to avoid left in it.
  • Deleting your account (in the app under Settings, Account, Delete account, or by emailing privacy@korvi.eu) happens straight away and can't be undone. It deletes your account (your name, email, profile picture and how you sign in), your place in your household with the foods you avoid, your private recipes and drafts, and your records of moving between households.
    • If other people with an account are in your household, it carries on: the one who has been in it longest takes it over, and it keeps its meals, lists, Regulars, the people in it and the recipes you wrote for it, without your name. If its name was made from your name, it's renamed.
    • If you were the only person with an account in it, the household is deleted with everything in it: the people you added and the foods they avoid, its meals, lists, Regulars and the recipes you wrote for it. Households you left earlier that nobody else is in are deleted too.
    • Public recipes you shared that we approved stay in Korvi without your name (credited, if Korvi shows a credit, to "a Korvi user").
    • For people you added who stay with a household, we keep when consent was given for the foods they avoid and which wording was shown, but no longer who gave it.
    • Recipe reports you made stay, without your note or your name. Ingredient requests and reviewed translation suggestions you made stay, without your name.
    • Feedback you sent stays in our GitHub repository with its text and your account ID, which no longer links to anyone. We remove any email address from it; GitHub's edit history keeps an earlier version unless we delete it by hand.
    • Deleting your account doesn't cancel a Korvi Plus subscription: cancel it in the App Store or Google Play.
    • If you signed in with Apple, we also ask Apple to remove Korvi's access to your Apple ID (on an iPhone; elsewhere, the app tells you how to remove it yourself).
  • Korvi Plus records. The full message the store sends us about a subscription event is emptied 90 days after it arrives; a short record of the event (its type, store, product and date) stays as the household's billing record. When a household is deleted, those records stop naming it.
  • Feedback is deleted, with its screenshot, 12 months after we've dealt with it.
  • Recipe reports are kept for 12 months after we've decided on them, then deleted; a report is also deleted if the recipe is.
  • Sign-in and email logs. Our database provider (Supabase) logs sign-ins with your email address, and our email provider (Resend) logs the emails it sends you (the address and subject). They keep these logs for [CHECK: Supabase and Resend log retention periods on our plans] and we can't delete them for one person.
  • Backups. Our database plan doesn't include backups. Before a major update we take one copy of the database ourselves, only so we can put data back if the update goes wrong. It's kept only in encrypted form, never in cloud storage or a code repository, and we delete it within 30 days of taking it at the latest. Data you delete can stay in such a copy until it's deleted.

Analytics and error reports are linked to your account ID, never your name or email, so they are pseudonymised, not anonymous. PostHog keeps analytics events for up to 1 year. Sentry keeps error reports for [CHECK: Sentry retention on our plan].

7. Your rights

Under the GDPR, you have the right to:

  • access the personal data we hold about you, and get a copy of it in a format you can take elsewhere;
  • have it corrected if it's wrong, or deleted;
  • ask us to restrict how we use it;
  • object to our using it where we rely on our legitimate interests (for example error reports or feedback);
  • withdraw your consent at any time, where we rely on it: for the foods you avoid, by clearing the list (see "People you cook for and food you avoid"), and for analytics, in Settings. Withdrawing doesn't affect what we did before.

To use these rights, email privacy@korvi.eu. We answer within one month. You can also complain to the Estonian Data Protection Inspectorate (Andmekaitse Inspektsioon, www.aki.ee), or to the data protection authority where you live or work.

8. Children

You must be 18 or over to create a Korvi account. When you sign in for the first time, you confirm you're 18 or over. We don't knowingly let anyone younger sign up, and if we learn that someone under 18 has an account we'll delete it. A parent or carer can add a child to their household so Korvi plans for them: that stores only that they're a child, an optional first name and the foods they avoid, entered by the adult, who confirms they're the child's parent or carer when adding foods the child avoids. A child added this way has no account and can't use Korvi. The adult can change or remove the child's details at any time, as above, or email privacy@korvi.eu.
